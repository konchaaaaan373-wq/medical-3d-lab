## 1. What question this model answers

**Where does one Couinaud segment stop and the next begin, and what runs
between them rather than inside them?**

## 2. What it is

An interactive gross-anatomy model of the liver, built in code. One
procedurally shaped liver is cut on the planes Couinaud's scheme names into
**nine parts** (I, II, III, IVa, IVb, V, VI, VII, VIII) whose union is the
organ. Through them run the two vascular trees: the three **hepatic veins** on
the very planes that divide the segments, and the **portal pedicles** inside
them. The gallbladder hangs in its fossa. **27 structures are selectable.**

The distinction the scene exists for: **a hepatic vein runs between segments
and a portal pedicle runs within one.** That is why a segment can be removed
without cutting anything belonging to its neighbours, and why a surgeon finds a
resection plane by following a hepatic vein.

The second distinction, which the copy repeats where a reader is looking at
either structure: **the falciform ligament is not the boundary between the
right and left liver.** Cantlie's line is, and it runs through the middle
hepatic vein where nothing on the surface marks it.

## 3. What it is not

- **Not a specimen shape.** The outer form is a warped ellipsoid with a liver's
  proportions. There is no porta hepatis notch of its own, no bare area, no
  ligamentous attachments and no caudate process.
- **Not a biliary model.** No intrahepatic ducts, no common bile duct, no cystic
  duct. The gallbladder is a shape with no volume in millilitres.
- **Not an arterial model.** The hepatic artery is not drawn at all.
- **Not a volumetry tool.** Segment volume shares are reference proportions used
  to check the partition, not a measurement of anybody's liver.
- **Not solved.** No portal pressure, no flow, no state. `portal-hypertension`
  and `liver-portal-flow` are where a solved circulation lives, and no scene
  draws both that tree and this one.

## 4. Sources and licence

All geometry is generated by this repository from the plane definitions in
`liverAnatomy.js`. No external 3D asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the tests hold: nine parts partitioning the parenchyma with no gap and no
overlap; five sectors grouping them; each segment's share of the whole within
tolerance of its reference proportion; each hepatic vein lying on the plane it
is named for; Cantlie's line held apart from the falciform ligament; segment I
taking a pedicle from each side and draining straight to the cava.

What is not held: the outer contour, the position of the organ relative to the
ribs or the diaphragm, and any dimension in centimetres.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene says what frame shape it needs.** The widest whole-liver view
  fills the frame's width at an aspect of 0.87, so a narrow window pulls the
  camera back rather than cropping the organ. The number is measured from the
  parenchyma, so it follows the shape rather than being retuned beside it.
- **Nine distinguishable colours, not nine shades of liver.** The builder's own
  segment colours are what the organ looks like, and at that separation nobody
  can see where VII stops and VIII starts. A boundary nobody can see is not a
  boundary the scene has shown, so the default mode separates them and "Natural
  tissue" puts the liver's colour back. A colour is not a claim about tissue.
- **The layer slider fades the parenchyma** so the two trees can be seen through
  it, in place. Outflow comes up first, inflow second, because that is the order
  the distinction is easiest to read in.
- **The transverse section cuts.** Different from the fade, and labelled as a
  cut.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — resection, donor evaluation, ablation or volumetry included.
