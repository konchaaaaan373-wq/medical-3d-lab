## 1. What question this model answers

**If the socket is that small, what is holding the arm on?**

## 2. What it is

A **right shoulder** with the arm at the side. The **scapula** is a thin plate
on the back of the chest; at its outer angle is the **glenoid**, and the
**glenoid labrum** is the rim that deepens it. Against it sits the **head of the
humerus** — far larger than the socket — with the **greater** and **lesser
tubercles** below it and the **shaft** hanging from it. Above the joint, the
**acromion** and the **coracoid process** stand out from the scapula and are
joined by the **coracoacromial ligament** into an arch; the **clavicle** joins
the whole thing to the trunk.

Between the head and that arch runs the **rotator cuff**: **supraspinatus** over
the top, **infraspinatus** and **teres minor** behind, **subscapularis** in
front — three to the greater tubercle and one to the lesser. The **long head of
biceps** begins inside the joint on the socket rim and turns down the groove
between the two tubercles. **Articular cartilage** covers both surfaces, and the
**inferior glenohumeral ligament** slings under the head.
**20 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** One position, arm at the side. The cuff neither tightens
  nor slackens, the head does not translate, and no ligament changes length.
- **No dimension is a measurement.** Bone shapes are simplified solids,
  cartilage thickness is drawn to be visible, and no attachment footprint is a
  measured one.
- **Each cuff tendon is one strap** standing for a muscle and its tendon. The
  myotendinous junctions, the muscle bellies and the footprints are not drawn.
- **The subacromial space is drawn wider than it is.** In life it is a few
  millimetres against a head of several centimetres; here it is opened up until
  the tendon under the arch can be seen and clicked. It is a *display* value
  (`SUBACROMIAL_DISPLAY_GAP`), not a dimension of the anatomy, and **no
  clearance may be read off this model**.
- **Glenoid version and humeral retroversion are not represented.**
- **No disease.** No tear, impingement, instability or degeneration in any form.
  That supraspinatus passes under a bony shelf is stated as anatomy and nothing
  more.
- **Not present:** the joint capsule apart from one ligament, the subacromial
  and other bursae, deltoid, the remaining scapular muscles, the axillary
  vessels, the brachial plexus.
- **One shoulder, the right one**, and not anyone's.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **what runs between what**, and that is what the tests
fix: the glenoid's articular face is a fraction of the head it faces; three cuff
tendons end on the greater tubercle and subscapularis on the lesser; the cuff
reaches the head from four different directions; supraspinatus passes under the
acromion rather than over it; the biceps tendon begins at the socket rim, ends
below the tubercles and runs between them on the way; and the coracoacromial
ligament spans coracoid to acromion above the cuff.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider fades the bones, and the humerus fades first and furthest**,
  because the cuff is what is behind it. Nothing is moved to be seen.
- **"The socket, with the humerus put away"** hides the humerus by tag rather
  than displacing it — the socket is behind the head, and a head that has been
  hidden still tells the truth about where the socket is.
- **"Under the arch"** exists because the subacromial space is a *gap*, not a
  structure: the only way to show it is to look from where it can be seen. The
  gap itself is **drawn open**, as a display value named as one — the
  alternative at this scale is a tendon nobody can see against a shelf nobody
  can tell it apart from.
- **"The cuff on its own"** puts every bone away, so the four tendons read as
  the sleeve they are rather than as four separate straps.
- **The opening view is anterolateral**: from straight in front the head hides
  the socket, and from straight lateral the scapula is edge-on.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5),
  so in this right shoulder medial is `+x`; every side comes from one `MEDIAL`
  constant.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — injection, arthroscopy, cuff repair, stabilisation or arthroplasty
included. It carries no grade, no test and no threshold.
