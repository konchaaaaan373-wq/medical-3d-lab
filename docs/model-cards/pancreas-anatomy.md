## 1. What question this model answers

**Which part of the pancreas is this, where does its duct run, and where is the
endocrine tissue?**

## 2. What it is

The pancreas is drawn as one gland along an axis, with a calibre profile that
already names head, neck, body and tail. This scene cuts that same axis into
those four, keeps the **main pancreatic duct** running the length of them, adds
the **islets** scattered through the gland, and shows the **duodenum** whose C
the head sits inside. **7 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **The islets are an arrangement, not a count.** Fourteen spheres placed from
  a fixed seed stand for roughly a million islets, which together are one or two
  per cent of the gland's mass. Their number and their size are chosen so they
  can be seen.
- **Not present:** uncinate process, accessory duct, common bile duct through
  the head, ampulla or papilla, acini, the splenic and mesenteric vessels the
  neck and body lie against.
- **No secretion.** Nothing flows and nothing is solved;
  `pancreatic-secretion` is where that lives.
- **The gallbladder is not here.** It hangs in its fossa on the liver, and is
  part of `liver-anatomy`. No bile duct is modelled anywhere in this repository
  yet.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

The part division is held through the anatomy contract in
`tests/organ-anatomy-scenes.test.js`: each named part is its own mesh, the tree
and the model agree, and the union is the gland that was there before. The
relationship the scene most depends on — the head inside the duodenal C — is
held by the shared placement in `tests/organ-anatomy.test.js`.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene says what frame shape it needs, and what it is about.** The
  gland is the widest subject of the six relative to its height, and the
  framing is set on the gland: the duodenum and spleen beside it are context
  and may crop. "Head and duodenum" is the viewpoint that goes in close.
- **The duodenum's two ends are closed as domes** rather than left as flat
  discs, which read as cut pipe on either side of the pancreatic head.
- **The gland is opaque at the start of the slider**, unlike the secretion
  scene's, where the duct is the subject from the first frame. Here the parts
  are the subject first and the duct and islets come up through them.
- **Colour separates the four parts**; "Natural tissue" puts one glandular
  colour back and takes the islets down to tissue as well.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — ERCP, biopsy, resection or staging included.
