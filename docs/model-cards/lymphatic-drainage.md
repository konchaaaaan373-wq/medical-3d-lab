## 1. What question this model answers

**If something spreads in lymph, where does it turn up?**

## 2. What it is

The lymphatic system as **routes and groups**, at body scale, inside a body
silhouette drawn for scale. The **thoracic duct** runs from the **cisterna
chyli** in the abdomen up behind the chest and across to the left venous angle;
the **right lymphatic duct** is short and takes a quarter of the body. The
**cervical**, **axillary** and **inguinal** node groups are drawn paired about
the midline, and two representative **routes** show the long way up and the
short way in. **9 structures are selectable**, including the silhouette.

**This is a companion scene.** One node at its own scale is
`lymph-node-anatomy`.

## 3. What it is not

- **Each node group is a marker, not a model of a node.** A real group is a
  dozen or more nodes of a few millimetres; these are beads big enough to click
  (`NODE_DISPLAY_SIZE`). **No node size or count may be read off this model.**
- **Not a map.** The great majority of lymphatic vessels and node groups —
  mediastinal, abdominal, pelvic, popliteal and the rest — are not drawn, and
  neither are the spleen, thymus or tonsils.
- **Nothing flows.** No direction is animated.
- **The course of the thoracic duct varies between people** in ways this does
  not represent, and no length, calibre or position is a measurement.
- **The silhouette is context for scale only**, with the limbs left off.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's lymphatic system.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the asymmetry and where the three palpable groups
are**, and that is what the tests fix: the thoracic duct is more than four
times the right duct's vertical span, starts in the abdomen and ends on the
patient's left; the right duct stays on the right and in the upper body; both
end at about the same height; the cisterna chyli is at the thoracic duct's
lower end; and the three groups are paired about the midline at neck, axilla
and groin height, with the axillary ones furthest from the midline.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The body silhouette is context**, excluded from the framing subject and
  faded by the slider — the point of a drainage map is where things are *in a
  person*, and the outline should never decide the zoom.
- **Two side views exist**, one per duct, because the asymmetry is the subject
  and it cannot be seen from the front alone.
- **Each group is one structure with a dozen beads**, because "the axillary
  nodes" is what a reader asks about and what a report names.
- **The routes appear with the slider**, so the first thing a reader sees is
  where the nodes are rather than a web of lines.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, staging, or planning
any biopsy, dissection or radiotherapy field.
