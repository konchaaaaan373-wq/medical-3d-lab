## 1. What question this model answers

**What holds these two bones together, and what is between them?**

## 2. What it is

A **right knee**, drawn in full extension. The **femur** ends in two condyles,
and the gap between them is the intercondylar notch — not drawn as a structure,
because it is not one: it is where both cruciates run. Below them the two
**tibial plateaus** sit on the **tibia**, with the **fibula** to the lateral
side carrying no weight, and the **patella** in front, a bone inside a tendon.

**Articular cartilage** covers every surface that meets another, so the two
bones never touch. Between them the two **menisci** deepen a flat plateau into
a socket. Four ligaments decide what the joint will and will not do: the
**anterior** and **posterior cruciates** crossing inside the notch, and the
**medial** and **lateral collaterals** down the outside of each side. The
**quadriceps** and **patellar tendons** cross the front as one chain with the
patella in the middle of it. **17 structures are selectable.**

This is the first joint in the catalogue, and it is a different kind of subject
from an organ. An organ scene answers *what part of it is this*; a joint scene
answers *what is this attached to at both ends, and what does that arrangement
mean*. Every description here names both ends.

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** The joint is drawn at one position. Rolling, gliding, the
  screw-home rotation and patellar tracking are not represented, and no
  ligament tightens or slackens.
- **No dimension is a measurement.** No length, angle, thickness or attachment
  footprint. Bone shapes are simplified solids, and **cartilage thickness is
  drawn to be visible**, not to scale.
- **Each ligament is one band between two points.** The separate bundles of the
  cruciates, which tighten at different angles, and the superficial and deep
  layers of the medial collateral are not modelled.
- **No injury.** No tear, degeneration, effusion or repair is represented in any
  form. Where a ligament runs and what it resists is stated as anatomy, and
  nothing more.
- **Not present:** the joint capsule, the synovium with its bursae and the
  suprapatellar pouch, the popliteus, the rest of the posterolateral corner,
  the hamstring and iliotibial attachments, the meniscal horns as separate
  attachments, the popliteal vessels and the nerves.
- **One knee, the right one**, and not anyone's.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **which structure runs between which two points**, and
that is what the tests fix: the two cruciates cross inside the notch between the
condyles and run in opposite anteroposterior directions; each collateral lies
outside both condyles on its own side; the lateral collateral ends on the fibula
and the medial does not; each meniscus sits between its own condyle and its own
plateau; the cartilage layer stands off every bone surface it covers; and the
extensor mechanism is one chain from above the femur to the tibial tuberosity
with the patella in it.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider fades the bones**, because everything a reader is here for is
  behind one. Nothing is moved to be seen: the cruciates stay inside the notch
  and are reached by making the condyles transparent.
- **A "Ligaments and menisci only" viewpoint exists**, which puts every bone
  away so the four ligaments read as the arrangement they are — two crossing in
  the middle, one down each side.
- **The section cuts the femur away rather than moving it aside.** "The plateau
  from above" is the view the two menisci are actually legible in, and it is
  reached by a cut at the joint line — not by lifting the femur off.
- **The opening view is an anterior oblique**, because straight from the front
  the two condyles overlap into one mass and from an angle they read as two.
- **The two condyles and the two plateaus are coloured apart** in "By structure"
  so medial and lateral can be told at a glance; "Natural tissue" puts bone
  colour back and leaves the soft tissues distinguishable.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5),
  so in this right knee the medial side is `+x` and the fibula is at `−x`. Every
  side in the builder is derived from one `MEDIAL` constant.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **The shafts are drawn and are not framed to.** The femur, the tibia and the
  fibula run out of the scene, and fitting the whole of them pushed the camera
  back far enough that the structures a reader can click crowded into a single
  column. They are tagged as context, so the camera measures itself against the
  joint — condyles, plateaus, patella, menisci, ligaments and the extensor
  mechanism — and the bones still run off the edges as they should: a reader
  tells a knee from an elbow partly by which way its bones go. **Nothing is
  hidden and nothing is shortened**; only the box the camera fits to is
  narrower. Measured: a click tour that could name three structures, all in one
  column, now names four across three columns and three rows.
  This scene is a **tall** subject and is framed as one — it reaches across 8 of
  9 sampled rows where the published organs reach 4 or 5, and its widest point
  is 0.16 of the frame against their 0.28–0.32. That is a shape, not a defect;
  F-134 was closed on it.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — injection, arthroscopy, ligament reconstruction, meniscal surgery or
arthroplasty included. It carries no grade, no test and no threshold.
