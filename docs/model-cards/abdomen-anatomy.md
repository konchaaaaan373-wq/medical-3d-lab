## 1. What question this model answers

**In the bag, or behind it?** One line sorts every organ in an abdomen.

## 2. What it is

A **region**, not an organ. The **abdominal wall** and the **rectus** straps in
it; the **lumbar vertebrae** and **psoas** behind. The **peritoneal cavity**
with the **greater omentum** and the **mesentery** in it, and the
**retroperitoneum** behind it. In the bag: **liver**, **stomach**, **spleen**,
**small bowel** and two of the four lengths of **colon**. Behind it: the
**pancreas** and **duodenum** (each with a part in the bag), the **kidneys**,
**adrenal glands** and **ureters**, and the other two lengths of colon. Crossing
the back wall: the **aorta**, the **inferior vena cava**, the **coeliac trunk**,
the **superior mesenteric vessels**, the **inferior mesenteric artery** and the
**renal vessels**. **24 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** Nothing is digested, nothing peristalses, no organ fills or
  empties, and **no pressure exists anywhere in this model**.
- **Every organ is an outline in its place, and none of them is an atlas.** The
  liver, kidney, pancreas, stomach, colon and adrenal each have their own scene
  at their own scale; nothing about their internal structure is drawn here. The
  adrenal's cortex and medulla — two organs in one, which is the point of that
  gland — are named and not drawn.
- **No dimension, calibre, angle or volume is a measurement.**
- **Not present:** the two layers of peritoneum, the lesser sac and the opening
  into it, the fascial layers of the retroperitoneum — which are what actually
  decide where a leak spreads — the three flat muscles of the wall, the linea
  alba, the rectus sheath, the inguinal canal, the gallbladder and biliary tree,
  the caecum, appendix and rectum, the portal vein, the lymphatics and nodes,
  the nerves, and **every branch of every artery drawn here**. Accessory renal
  arteries, which are common, are not drawn.
- **In a woman the peritoneal cavity is not quite closed.** That is not
  represented.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's abdomen.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **which side of the peritoneum each structure is on**,
and that is what the tests fix, against the same function the geometry is built
from: liver, stomach, spleen and small bowel are wholly in front of the line;
kidneys, adrenals, ureters, aorta and cava wholly behind it; **the pancreas and
the duodenum have parts on both sides**, at 81% and 68% behind; and the colon's
four lengths come out two wholly behind and two wholly in front. Also fixed: the
right kidney sits lower than the left and both lie lateral to and in front of
psoas; the cava is to the patient's right of the midline and the aorta to the
left; the three ventral branches leave at three descending levels and all three
start on the aorta; and **the superior mesenteric artery passes in front of the
third part of the duodenum**.

"Wholly" is 96% of a structure's surface rather than all of it. The missing few
per cent are the model's own thickness — a vessel is a tube with a wall, and its
path is a smoothed curve that overshoots slightly between the points it is
written from, so a little of its far surface can sit across a line its centre
never approaches. The straddling organs are at 45–90%, so nothing is near that
threshold by accident.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene opens in Natural** (2026-09-21). It used to open in **Regions**,
  because that mode is first in the list and the list is ordered by what the
  scene is for. A reader looking at the landing hero said the heart did not
  look like an organ, and they were right: colour that carries no anatomical
  claim is still the first claim a picture makes. Regions is one press away
  and nothing about it changed. The brain is the exception and still opens
  colour-coded — 147 parcels cannot be told apart in tissue colour.
- **The slider takes away the wall**, which is the one part that can be removed
  without moving anything.
- **Two viewpoints are a pair and carry the scene**: *in the bag* and *behind
  the bag*, each with the other side put away. They are not two pictures of
  different things; they are the same abdomen sorted by one line.
- **`peritoneumBackAt(y)` is the boundary of two structures at once.** The back
  wall of the peritoneal cavity and the front wall of the retroperitoneum are
  the same surface, so nothing in the model can be in both or in neither
  (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1). Every organ is then held on one side of
  it by `keepSideOfPeritoneum`, so the claim about each organ is a property of
  the geometry rather than a label attached to it.
- **`aortaAt(y)` places the aorta and every branch that leaves it**, so a branch
  cannot start off its own vessel; `psoasAt(y, side)` is the shelf both the
  kidney and the ureter are placed against.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  every paired structure comes from one `LEFT` constant.
- **Laid out in centimetres and scaled once** (`WORLD_SCALE`), because the
  shared viewer clamps the camera (`docs/follow-ups.md` F-90).
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, **interpreting an
image**, or planning or performing any incision, puncture, drain or operation.
