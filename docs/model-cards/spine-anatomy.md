## 1. What question this model answers

**Which region, and what is one segment made of?**

## 2. What it is

A whole column in four regions — **cervical**, **thoracic**, **lumbar** and the
**sacrum** — built from one curve, so the neck goes forward, the chest back and
the low back forward again. One lumbar level is drawn in full: **vertebral
body**, two **pedicles**, two **laminae**, four **facet joints** and the
**spinous process**, with the disc below it as **annulus fibrosus** and
**nucleus pulposus**. The **spinal canal** runs behind the bodies the whole
way; the **spinal cord** stops partway down and the **cauda equina** continues
below it; one pair of **nerve roots** leaves beneath the pedicles of the
detailed level. **15 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **One level is drawn in full and the rest are blocks.** The individual
  vertebrae of a region are not separately selectable.
- **Nothing moves or bends.** No flexion, no extension, no instability.
- **No height, width, angle or curve is a measurement.** The degree of each
  curve is drawn to read.
- **Not present:** the ribs and their joints, the ligaments of the column
  including the ligamentum flavum, the muscles, the intervertebral foramina as
  openings rather than gaps, the epidural fat and venous plexus, the coccyx,
  and the roots of every level except one pair.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's spine.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the order of the regions, the alternation of the
curves and the arrangement of one segment**, and that is what the tests fix:
the four regions stack in order with seven cervical and twelve thoracic levels;
the column leans forward at the neck, back at the chest and forward at the low
back; the canal runs behind the bodies for most of the column; the cord stops
well above the bottom of the canal and the cauda equina takes over where it
ends; the disc sits below the body with the nucleus inside the annulus; and the
pedicles run back from the body, the laminae close the arch behind them, the
spinous process is behind those, and the roots leave laterally beneath the
pedicles.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The detailed level stays in the column, in its place, at the same scale.**
  Enlarging it or lifting it out would make the scene easier to read and would
  also make it a diagram of two things rather than a model of one, so the way to
  it is a viewpoint.
- **A lateral view exists** because the three curves do not exist in any other
  one.
- **The canal is see-through at rest**, so the cord and the roots can be
  followed through it without waiting for the slider.
- **The slider fades the bone**, which is what the canal and its contents are
  inside.
- **Each region is one structure** however many blocks it is drawn as, because
  "the lumbar spine" is what a reader asks about.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, counting a level on
an image, or planning any injection, instrumentation or surgery.
