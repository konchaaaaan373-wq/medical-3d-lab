## 1. What question this model answers

**What shape is the wrist, and what has to share the space inside it?**

## 2. What it is

A right hand in anatomical position. The **radius** and **ulna** come down to
the wrist; the eight carpal bones — **scaphoid**, **lunate**, **triquetrum**,
**pisiform**, **trapezium**, **trapezoid**, **capitate**, **hamate** — are each
named individually, with the **hook of the hamate** drawn separately because it
is one of the two pillars the roof is anchored to. Out from there, the
**metacarpals** and the **proximal**, **middle** and **distal phalanges**. The
**flexor retinaculum** spans the arch of the carpus, and the **carpal tunnel**
is the space it leaves; the **flexor tendons** and the **median nerve** run
through it, the **extensor tendons** run over the back where there is no tunnel
at all, and the **thenar muscles** make the mound at the base of the thumb. **21
structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** No joint bends, no tendon slides, no thumb opposes and no
  grip is represented.
- **Bones are smooth blocks and shafts.** Heads, bases, styloids, joint
  surfaces, capsules and ligaments are not drawn, and the **saddle shape of the
  thumb's carpometacarpal joint** — which is what a thumb's opposition depends
  on — is described in words and not modelled.
- **The blood supply of the scaphoid is not represented at all**, which is the
  one thing that makes that bone matter more than its neighbours.
- **Seven cords stand for nine flexor tendons**, as a bundle: which tendon is
  which is not represented, and their sheaths are not drawn.
- **No bone length, joint angle, tendon calibre or tunnel dimension is a
  measurement**, and **no cross-sectional area or pressure is represented** — so
  nothing here supports any claim about the tunnel being tight.
- **Not present:** the extensor retinaculum and hoods, the thumb's extensors,
  the ulnar nerve and artery in their own canal, the intrinsic muscles other
  than the thenar mass, the skin and the nails.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's hand.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

The model claims an arrangement and the tests fix it: the eight carpals lie in
two rows in the right order across the wrist, and the **pisiform sits palmar to
the triquetrum** rather than beside it; the retinaculum spans the two pillars
and the tunnel lies under it and above the arch; the **median nerve is the most
palmar thing in the tunnel** with the flexor tendons deep to it; the extensor
tendons are dorsal to every bone; and the rays carry **five metacarpals, five
proximal phalanges, four middle phalanges and five distal phalanges** — the
thumb having two bones where every other digit has three.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider fades the bones**, which are what everything soft runs under or
  over. Nothing is moved out from under them.
- **One view carries the scene**: `across-the-tunnel`, end on down the wrist.
  The tunnel is a cross-section problem — an arch with a lid — and from any
  other angle it is a line.
- **The band and the space under it do not fade**, because they are what the
  slider is clearing the way to.
- **One table, four uses.** `RAYS` holds each ray's base, direction and bone
  lengths; the metacarpals and all three rows of phalanges are built from it,
  so a ray cannot have its bones in different places in different rows
  (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1). The thumb's missing middle phalanx is
  an absent entry in that table, so the model cannot accidentally grow a fifth.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  in this right hand with the palm forward, radial is `−x`, from one `RADIAL`
  constant.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure. It carries no joint surfaces, no ligaments, no blood supply and no
dimensions.
