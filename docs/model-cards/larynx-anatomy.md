## 1. What question this model answers

**Where do air and food share a space, and what keeps them apart again?**

## 2. What it is

One pharyngeal lumen, named in three lengths — **nasopharynx**,
**oropharynx**, **laryngopharynx** — with the **soft palate** hanging into it
and the **palatine tonsils** in its side walls. Below the laryngeal inlet the
larynx takes the middle of its front wall, and what is left of the lumen beside
it is the two **piriform sinuses**. The larynx itself: the **epiglottis**
standing over its door, the **hyoid bone** it hangs from, the **thyroid
cartilage** shield, the **cricoid cartilage** ring, the two **arytenoid
cartilages** on the back of that ring, and the **cricothyroid membrane** between
the two big cartilages in front. Inside it, the **vestibular folds**, the
**laryngeal ventricles**, the **vocal folds** and the **subglottic space**.
Below, the **trachea**, the **oesophagus** behind it, and a **recurrent
laryngeal nerve** in the groove between them on each side. **19 structures are
selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** Nothing is swallowed, the soft palate does not lift, the
  larynx does not rise, the epiglottis does not fold, and the vocal folds
  neither open, close nor vibrate. **The mechanism every structure here exists
  for is described and not shown.**
- **The glottis is drawn at one fixed opening** (`GLOTTIS_DISPLAY_GAP`), so that
  both folds and the gap between them can be seen and selected. **No airway
  calibre may be read off it.** That the two folds meet at the front is not a
  display choice: that end is the anterior commissure.
- **The cricothyroid membrane is a landmark only.** No route, depth, angle or
  technique is represented and **nothing here may be used to plan a procedure**.
- **No dimension, angle or calibre is a measurement.** That the subglottis is
  the narrowest point in a small child and the glottis in an adult is a fact
  about airways; **no age difference is drawn** — it is stated in words.
- **The two recurrent laryngeal nerves are drawn alike**, where in life they do
  not take the same course to reach the larynx; the left loops much lower,
  around the aortic arch, which is outside this scene.
- **Not present:** the tongue and valleculae, the thyroid gland, every muscle of
  the larynx and pharynx, the joints between the cartilages, the aryepiglottic
  folds, the vessels of the neck, and the layers of a vocal fold.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's larynx.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the arrangement of the crossing**, and that is what
the tests fix: the three named lengths of the pharynx stack in order without
overlapping; the piriform gutters reach forward past the larynx on both sides
while the laryngopharynx stays behind it; the two pairs of folds are separated
by the ventricle with the true folds below; the glottis closes to the midline in
front and opens behind, so the opening is a V; the cricothyroid membrane lies in
the gap between the thyroid and the cricoid with nothing else in front of it;
and the oesophagus lies behind the trachea with a nerve in the groove between
them on each side.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider fades the pharynx**, because the pharynx is the bag everything
  else is inside. Nothing is moved out of it.
- **The piriform gutters do not fade with the rest of the pharynx**, because
  they are the answer to the scene's question and a reader following a swallow
  needs them all the way down.
- **The viewpoints are the ones this anatomy is actually looked at from**: from
  behind, which is how a pharynx is opened; from above, which is how a larynx is
  looked at in life; and cut on the midline, which is how the crossing is drawn.
- **The cartilages are part-transparent at rest**, because everything the scene
  is about is inside them.
- **One function, two uses.** `laryngealWallAt(z)` is how far from the midline
  the inside of the larynx is; the thyroid cartilage is lathed about it and the
  folds attach to it, so a fold cannot end short of the cartilage or push
  through it (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1). It is also what makes the
  glottis a V.
- **One lumen, five names.** The three lengths of pharynx and the two gutters
  are all cut out of `pharynxSection`, so they cannot drift apart.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  every paired structure comes from one `LEFT` constant.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — **including any airway procedure**. The cricothyroid membrane is
drawn because it is a landmark worth being able to point at, not because this
model knows anything about reaching it.
