## 1. What question this model answers

**One axis with two different joints on it — and why the ligaments that hold it
never go slack.**

## 2. What it is

A right elbow. The **humerus**, flaring into a triangle that carries the
**trochlea**, the **capitellum** and the **medial** and **lateral
epicondyles**. The **olecranon** with its trochlear notch, the **ulna**, the
**radial head** and the **radius**. **Articular cartilage** over the surfaces
that move, and **one joint capsule** over both joints. The **ulnar** and
**radial collateral ligaments** and the **annular ligament**. The **biceps**
and **triceps tendons** and the **common flexor** and **common extensor
origins**. Crossing all of it, the **ulnar nerve**, the **median nerve** and the
**brachial artery**. **21 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** The joint does not bend, the forearm does not turn, the
  radial head does not spin and no tendon shortens. **Every structure here
  exists for a movement that is described and not shown.**
- **The articular cartilage is drawn thicker than life** so that it can be seen
  and clicked. No thickness may be read off it.
- **The capitellum is drawn as a whole sphere**, where in life it has no
  articular surface behind — so a radial head has nothing under it in full
  extension, which this model does not show.
- **Muscles are two common origins, not bellies.** No forearm muscle is drawn.
- **The interosseous membrane is not drawn at all** — and that is how load
  actually reaches the elbow from the hand, so the absence matters.
- **Each nerve is one cord on the usual course.** Branches are not drawn, and an
  ulnar nerve that slips forward over the epicondyle in flexion is not
  represented.
- **No dimension, angle, thickness or attachment footprint is a measurement**,
  and the carrying angle is not drawn as an angle at all.
- **Not present:** the growth plates, the bursae, the fat pads, the synovium,
  the veins of the cubital fossa, the bicipital aponeurosis, the lateral ulnar
  collateral ligament as its own band, the supinator crest, and every branch of
  every nerve and vessel.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's elbow.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the arrangement about one axis**, and that is what
the tests fix: the trochlea is waisted against both of its flanges and the
medial flange is the deeper; the capitellum is lateral to the trochlea and
centred on the same axis; no part of the ulna lies inside the trochlea and the
notch touches it; that notch **wraps past half a circle**, which is why the ulna
stays on without help; **both collateral ligaments start on the axis itself**;
the annular ligament reaches the ulna and passes outside the radial head while
the radial collateral ligament ends on the ring and never reaches the radius;
the ulnar nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle rather than in front of it;
and in the cubital fossa the biceps tendon, the brachial artery and the median
nerve lie in that order from the thumb side inwards.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider takes away the capsule**, because the capsule is the bag both
  joints are inside and the only thing here that can be removed without moving
  anything.
- **The viewpoints are the four sides a joint is examined from**, plus two
  questions — what the axis looks like with nothing on it, and what is lying in
  the hollow at the front — and one cut.
- **The cut is sagittal, through the trochlea**, because the C of the notch
  gripping the spool is the one thing about an elbow that no view from outside
  can show. The nerve, the artery and the muscle origins are hidden in that
  view: they are outside the joint and in front of the cut face.
- **One function, four uses.** `trochleaRadiusAt(x)` is how far the humeral
  articular surface is from the axis: the trochlea is lathed about it, the
  cartilage on it is lathed about it, **the ulna's notch is pressed onto it**,
  and the capsule is sized from it (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1). The
  notch therefore cannot gap from the spool or enter it.
- **`collateralOrigin(side)` puts both ligaments on the axis.** That they do not
  slacken as the elbow bends is a property of the construction rather than an
  agreement between two written coordinates.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  medial is `+x` in a right limb, as in `organs/shoulderJoint.js` and
  `organs/hand.js`, and every side comes from one `MEDIAL` constant.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning or
performing any **puncture, line, nerve block, reduction, injection or
operation**. The cubital tunnel, the radial head and the annular ligament are
drawn because they are landmarks worth being able to point at, not because this
model knows anything about reaching them or about reducing anything.
