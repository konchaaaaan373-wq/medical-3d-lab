## 1. What question this model answers

**What lies next to what in the neck — and why do the two nerves that move the
voice not take the same road?**

## 2. What it is

A **region**, not an organ. The **surface of the neck**, and under it four
muscle groups — **sternocleidomastoid**, **strap muscles**, **scalenes**,
**posterior neck muscles**. Behind everything, the **cervical vertebrae**. In
the midline, the **hyoid bone**, the **laryngeal cartilages**, the **trachea**,
and the **oesophagus** directly behind it. Moulded onto the airway, the
**thyroid lobes** and the **isthmus**, with four **parathyroid glands** on the
back of them. On each side, the **carotid sheath** and the three things inside
it — **common carotid artery**, **internal jugular vein**, **vagus nerve** —
with the **internal** and **external carotid arteries** above the division and a
chain of **deep cervical lymph nodes** along the vein. Finally the two
**recurrent laryngeal nerves**, and the **subclavian arteries** and **arch of
the aorta** they turn round. **23 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** Nothing is swallowed, the larynx does not rise, the thyroid
  does not move with it, no vessel pulses and no muscle contracts. **The one
  sign that tells a thyroid lump from every other lump in a neck is described
  and not shown.**
- **Three things are drawn larger than life** so that they can be seen and
  clicked, and the amount is declared in `DISPLAY` in the builder: the
  **parathyroid glands** (about 6 × 4 × 2 mm in life), the **vagus nerves**, and
  the **recurrent laryngeal nerves** (about 2 mm across). **No size may be read
  off any of them.**
- **Each recurrent laryngeal nerve is drawn as one cord on the usual course.**
  In life it branches before entering the larynx, may pass in front of or behind
  the inferior thyroid artery, and on the right may not recur at all. **The
  claim is that the two sides differ, not where either one is.**
- **Muscles are four groups, not bellies.** Sternohyoid, sternothyroid,
  thyrohyoid and omohyoid are one ribbon; anterior, middle and posterior scalene
  are one belly; trapezius, splenius and the suboccipital muscles are one mass.
  The triangles of the neck are described and not drawn.
- **The lymph nodes are one chain of five a side, at one size.** The **levels
  (I–VI) are not drawn or labelled**, and no node here is enlarged, normal or
  abnormal — size carries no meaning in this scene.
- **The top of the chest is context.** The arch and the subclavian arteries are
  drawn only as far as the nerves need in order to turn round them, and they are
  excluded from the scene's own framing bounds. **This is not a mediastinum.**
- **No dimension, calibre, angle or distance is a measurement**, and no neck is
  this shape.
- **Not present:** the brachial plexus, the phrenic nerve, the superior
  laryngeal nerve, the thyroid's own arteries and veins, the vertebral artery,
  the spinal cord, the fascial planes as layers, the skin, platysma, the
  brachiocephalic trunk as a named vessel, and every branch of the external
  carotid.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's neck.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the arrangement**, and that is what the tests fix:
the oesophagus lies behind the trachea and leans to the patient's left as it
descends; each thyroid lobe touches the air column and no part of it lies
inside; inside the sheath the artery is medial, the vein lateral and larger,
and the vagus behind both; above the division the external carotid runs in front
of the internal; both recurrent laryngeal nerves end in the tracheo-oesophageal
groove at the level of the cricoid; and **the left turns lower than the right**,
below the arch rather than at the root of the neck.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider removes the wrapping, in the order a neck is opened**: the
  surface first, then the muscle groups. Nothing inside is moved, because the
  whole point of the region is that its contents have nowhere else to be.
- **The carotid sheath is translucent at rest**, because the bundle is the unit
  and what is in it has to stay visible.
- **The viewpoints are the pictures this anatomy is drawn as**: from in front,
  which is how it is approached; from the side, which is the only direction the
  bundle's three contents are three things; from behind, which is how the
  grooves and the backs of the lobes are seen; cut across at the isthmus, looked
  at **from below**, which puts the patient's left on the right of the screen as
  an axial image is always read; and the two recurrent nerves together, which is
  what the scene is for.
- **One function, four uses.** `airwayAt(y)` is where the air column is and how
  wide: the trachea is built round it, the laryngeal cartilages are built round
  it, **each thyroid lobe's medial hollow is pressed against it**, and the
  groove the recurrent nerve climbs is measured from it
  (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1). The lobes therefore cannot gap from the
  trachea or enter it.
- **One table decides what is next to what.** `SHEATH.contents` holds the three
  offsets inside the sheath, written medial-first and multiplied by the side, so
  the artery is medial on both sides rather than medial on one.
- **The two recurrent nerves are written separately, not mirrored.** So are the
  two common carotids, whose origins differ, and the two vagus nerves, which
  leave the sheath at different heights. Mirroring any of them would delete the
  scene's subject.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  every paired structure comes from one `LEFT` constant.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.
- **A cut is of something, and may leave the rest whole.** A section plane
  removes whatever it crosses, which is right for the organ the cut is opening
  and wrong for anything that merely passes through the same plane: a tube
  curving away from it loses more of itself the further it goes, and what
  survives is a crescent of wall that reads as a flat blade rather than as a
  tube. So a viewpoint may name the parts its cut is **not** about. Those keep
  their whole geometry, are given no cut face, and stay clickable on both sides
  of the plane. Where a viewpoint names nothing, its cut is of everything it
  crosses, as before.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
**puncture, central line, nerve block, airway procedure or operation**. The
jugular vein, the cricoid, the isthmus and the scalene gap are drawn because
they are landmarks worth being able to point at, not because this model knows
anything about reaching them.
