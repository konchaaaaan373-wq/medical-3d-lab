## 1. What question this model answers

**What is behind the thyroid, and why does that decide what a thyroid operation
has to avoid?**

## 2. What it is

A gross-anatomy model of the thyroid, built in code. Two **lobes** and the
**isthmus** between them are clasped around the **trachea**; a **pyramidal
lobe** runs up from the isthmus. Behind the gland are the four **parathyroid
glands** and, on each side, the **recurrent laryngeal nerve** in the groove
between the trachea and the **oesophagus**. **12 structures are selectable.**

The slider fades the gland rather than removing it, because what is behind
something only means anything while the thing it is behind is still there.

## 3. What it is not

- **Not measured.** Every dimension is schematic. Lobe size, isthmus width and
  the gland's position on the trachea are drawn to be recognisable, not to be
  read off.
- **The parathyroid positions are plausible, not fixed.** Where they sit is the
  most variable thing in the anterior neck — which is the reason a surgeon looks
  for them rather than knowing where they are. Four are drawn because four is
  the usual number; between two and six occur.
- **The pyramidal lobe is present in about half of people.** This model always
  draws it. Do not read that as "everyone has one".
- **The nerves' course below the neck is not modelled.** Round the subclavian
  artery on the right and the aortic arch on the left is exactly the difference
  that makes the two sides not mirror images in life, and it is off the bottom
  of this model.
- **Not present:** thyroid and inferior thyroid arteries, veins, the pretracheal
  fascia and strap muscles, tracheal cartilage rings, larynx, the follicles
  (they are `thyroid-hormone`'s subject, at a different scale), any lymph node.
- **No function.** Nothing is secreted and nothing is solved here.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **arrangement**, and the arrangement is what the tests
fix: the gland is in front of the trachea and the nerves behind it; each nerve
is on its own side, lateral to the trachea and medial to its own lobe; the four
parathyroids are behind the gland, two upper and two lower. Those are held in
`tests/organ-parts-anatomy.test.js`. The part division and the bilingual naming
are held through the anatomy contract in `tests/organ-anatomy-scenes.test.js`.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The gland fades, the structures behind it appear.** Nothing is moved and
  nothing is removed; the gland is still there at the far end of the slider.
- **A "Posterior" viewpoint exists** because the parathyroids and the nerves
  cannot be seen from the front at all, and every one of those structures names
  it as the view it prefers.
- **The trachea and the oesophagus are context**, excluded from the framing
  subject. The oesophagus is drawn for one reason: without it the groove the
  nerve runs in is nothing at all.
- **Colour separates the parts**; "Natural tissue" puts one glandular colour
  back.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — thyroidectomy, parathyroid exploration, nerve monitoring or biopsy
included.
