## 1. What question this model answers

**What holds the arch of a foot up, and where does a foot actually turn?**

## 2. What it is

A right foot standing on a flat ground line. The **tibia** and **fibula** come
down on either side of the **talus** and grip it; the **calcaneus** is under it,
and in front the **navicular** rides high on the inside while the **cuboid**
sits low on the outside, with the **cuneiforms** between them and the forefoot.
The **metatarsals** and the three rows of phalanges make the five rays. The
**plantar fascia** is slung from the heel to the heads of the metatarsals and
the **spring ligament** sits right under the head of the talus. The **Achilles**,
**tibialis posterior** and **peroneal tendons** are drawn, with the **deltoid
ligament** as one sheet inside and the **lateral ligaments** as three separate
bands outside, and the **ankle** and **subtalar joints** are drawn as the spaces
they are. **20 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves and nothing bears weight.** No joint bends, no arch flattens,
  no tendon pulls and no toe lifts — so the windlass mechanism the copy
  describes is described and not shown.
- **Bones are smooth blocks and shafts.** Joint surfaces, capsules, the
  sesamoids, the fat pad under the heel and the tarsal tunnel are not drawn.
- **The three cuneiforms are drawn as one block**, so the wedge shape that makes
  the transverse arch is described rather than shown.
- **The blood supply of the talus is not represented at all**, which is the one
  thing that makes that bone matter more than its neighbours.
- **No bone length, arch height, joint angle or ligament strength is a
  measurement**, and no order of failure is represented.
- **Not present:** the muscles, the skin, the nails, the nerves and the vessels.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's foot.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

The model claims an arrangement and the tests fix it: the navicular rides higher
than the cuboid, so the arch is an arch and not a rhetorical flourish; the
plantar fascia runs from the heel to the metatarsal heads and passes **below**
the top of that arch, which is what makes it a bowstring; the talus lies between
the leg above and the calcaneus below with a joint space at each boundary; the
**lateral malleolus reaches lower than the medial one**; the deltoid is one
sheet where the lateral side is three separate bands; and the rays carry five
metatarsals, five proximal phalanges, **four** middle phalanges and five distal
phalanges.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene opens in Natural** (2026-09-21). It used to open in **Regions**,
  because that mode is first in the list and the list is ordered by what the
  scene is for. A reader said the heart on the landing page did not look like
  an organ, and the default changed everywhere but the brain. Regions is one
  press away and nothing about it changed. **This card did not move when the
  others did**: its source list omitted the base class every one of these
  scenes is built on, so the digest could not see a change to shared
  behaviour. The list is corrected.
- **The scene opens on the medial view**, because that is the only side the arch
  can be seen from. From the outside it is not there; from underneath it is a
  footprint.
- **The slider fades the bones**, which are what the band and the ligaments are
  behind. Nothing is moved out from under anything.
- **The band and the sling do not fade**, because they are what the slider is
  clearing the way to.
- **The joints are drawn as spaces**, because a joint is a relationship and not
  an object — and because the point of the subtalar joint is that it is a
  *second* one, under the first.
- **One table, four uses.** `RAYS` holds each ray's base, direction and bone
  lengths; the great toe's missing middle phalanx is an absent entry in it
  (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1).
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  in this right foot medial is `+x`, from one `MEDIAL` constant.
- **The foot is laid out in centimetres and drawn at `WORLD_SCALE`.** The shared
  viewer clamps the camera to 55 units from its target, and a foot seen from the
  side is nearly thirty units long: at life scale the framing asks for more
  distance than the viewer will give and the toes are cropped on a phone, with
  no error anywhere. The layout stays in the unit the anatomy is known in and
  the finished group is scaled. See F-90 in `docs/follow-ups.md`.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — including anything about gait, orthotics or load, all of which are
about a foot under weight, and nothing here bears any.
