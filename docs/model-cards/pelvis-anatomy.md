## 1. What question this model answers

**One gap in one muscle, and one crossing that has two names.**

## 2. What it is

A **region**, not an organ. The **pelvic ring** and the **pubic symphysis**; the
**levator ani** slung across the outlet and the **levator hiatus** in it; the
**pelvic peritoneum** draped over the top and the **peritoneal pouch** dipping
behind the bladder. Through and around the gap: **bladder**, **ureters**,
**urethra**, **rectum**, **anal canal** and **sigmoid colon**. The **common**,
**internal** and **external iliac** vessels. And both sets of reproductive
organs — **uterus, ovaries and tubes, vagina, uterine artery** and **prostate,
seminal vesicles, vas deferens**. **22 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **One model carries both sets, and no body has both.** Everything else is the
  same in both sexes and is drawn once; the scene opens on those shared parts,
  and each set has a viewpoint that shows it with the other put away. **That is
  a display arrangement and not an anatomy**, declared in `FEMALE_SET` /
  `MALE_SET`, here, and in the scene disclaimer. Nothing about either set's
  position is changed to make them coexist — which is why the test can check
  that they overlap.
- **Nothing moves.** Nothing fills or empties, no sphincter opens or closes, the
  floor does not contract, and **no pressure exists anywhere in this model**.
  The bladder is drawn **empty**, so the one thing that makes its position
  interesting — that a full bladder rises out of the pelvis in front of the
  peritoneum — is described and not shown.
- **The bony ring is one funnel**, and the difference between a male and a
  female ring, which is real and useful, is **not represented**.
- **No dimension, calibre, angle or volume is a measurement**, and the size of
  the pouch is not a capacity.
- **Every organ is an outline in its place and none is an atlas.**
  `bladder-anatomy`, `uterus-anatomy`, `prostate-anatomy` and
  `pelvic-floor-anatomy` are those structures at their own scale.
- **Not present:** the sacroiliac joints, the acetabulum, the ligaments of the
  pelvis, the three parts of the levator sling, both anal sphincters and the
  perineal body, the pelvic fascia, the ligaments that suspend the uterus, the
  nerves, the lymphatics, the veins beside every artery, and every branch of
  every artery except the one that makes the crossing.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's pelvis.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the arrangement**, and that is what the tests fix: on
each side the ureter passes below the bridge point and **both** the uterine
artery and the vas deferens pass through it and over the ureter, so the claim
cannot be true of one set and false of the other; the urethra, anal canal and
vagina all pass inside the gap while no part of the bladder, prostate or uterus
lies below the sling around it; the sheet begins exactly where the gap ends on
every ray, so the hole is an opening and not a dimple; the pouch is the lowest
point the peritoneum reaches and lies between the bladder in front and the
rectum behind; and the two sets of organs **overlap**, which is the geometric
form of "nobody has both".

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene opens on what is shared.** Not on either set — there is no neutral
  body with both, and opening on one would make the other look like an
  alternative rather than the same region.
- **`bridge` is the one view that shows both sets on purpose**, because it is
  one crossing with two names and seeing them together is the lesson.
- **The slider takes away the ring**, which is the only part that can be removed
  without moving anything.
- **`floorAt(x, z)` is the boundary between two countries.** The floor is built
  from it, the gap is cut in it, every organ in the true pelvis is held above
  it, and the perineum is measured down from it
  (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 1).
- **`bridgeAt(side)` is written once and used three times** — the ureter under
  it, the uterine artery through it, the vas deferens through it.
- **The gap is a real hole.** Each ray of the sheet starts where it leaves the
  gap; displacing the vertices inside it instead drew a funnel hanging through
  the floor, which is a spike and not an opening.
- **The ring is a ring, not a bowl.** A shell of revolution at these proportions
  reads as a plastic tub; what says "pelvis" is the brim with a wing flaring off
  it and an outlet below.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5);
  every paired structure comes from one `LEFT` constant.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.

## 7. What it must never be used for

**Any examination.** Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis,
or planning or performing any puncture, catheter, incision or operation.
