## 1. What question this model answers

**Where along the chain does sound get from air to fluid?**

## 2. What it is

A **right ear**, laid out along one line. The **auricle** collects and the
**external auditory canal** — which turns on the way in — carries, to the
**tympanic membrane**, drawn inwards at its centre by the bone on it. Across
the **middle ear cavity** go the **malleus**, **incus** and **stapes**, the last
of them with its footplate in the oval window; the **Eustachian tube** is the
cavity's only supply of air. Beyond the window, the **cochlea** — two and a half
tapering turns — the **vestibule**, and the three **semicircular canals** drawn
as one structure in three planes, with the **vestibulocochlear nerve** leaving
medially. **12 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** The drum does not vibrate, the chain does not conduct, and
  **there is no fluid in the model at all**.
- **Everything medial to the drum is drawn far larger than life.** An ossicle is
  a few millimetres against an auricle of sixty. It is a display scale
  (`DEEP_EAR_VISUAL_SCALE`), and **no size relation across the drum may be read
  off this model**.
- **No length, calibre, angle or turn-count is a measurement.** That there are
  about two and a half turns is a fact about cochleas; how large the spiral is
  drawn is not.
- **Not present:** the temporal bone and mastoid air cells, the facial nerve
  crossing the middle ear, the two ossicular muscles, the ampullae, the round
  window membrane, the scalae and the organ of Corti.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's ear.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the order along the chain and the three joins in
it**, and that is what the tests fix: auricle, canal, drum, cavity, vestibule
and nerve lie in that order going inwards; the canal ends at the drum and the
umbo is the drum's most medial point; malleus meets incus meets stapes and the
malleus does not reach the stapes; only the stapes is in the oval window; all
three ossicles cross the air space; the Eustachian tube leaves the cavity
forwards, downwards and inwards; the cochlea is a coil rather than a tube; and
the three canals each lie in a different plane.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **Three viewpoints in the order sound takes** — outer, middle, inner — each
  framing its part and hiding what is lateral to it by tag. Nothing is moved:
  the ossicles stay the size they are drawn and the camera comes closer.
- **The drum and the canal are part-transparent at rest**, because a reader has
  to see through them to follow the chain; neither fades with the slider, since
  neither ever started solid.
- **The slider fades the outer ear**, which is what the middle and inner ear are
  behind.
- **The three canals are one structure with three meshes**, because which of
  the three is which is a question about plane, not about identity.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5),
  so in this right ear medial is `+x`; every side comes from one `MEDIAL`
  constant.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure. It carries no audiogram, no pressure and no threshold.
