## 1. What question this model answers

**Which zone is this, and what runs through it?**

## 2. What it is

One chestnut-shaped gland cut into McNeal's four zones. The **peripheral zone**
is the *outside* of the gland — a shell, most of the glandular tissue, the
surface against the rectum. The **transition** and **central zones** are the
*inside*, separated by a plane through the **verumontanum**: transition round
the urethra above it, central the cone the ejaculatory ducts run down. The
**anterior fibromuscular stroma** is the front and is not glandular at all.

Through them run the **prostatic urethra**, from the **bladder neck** to the
apex, and the two **ejaculatory ducts**, formed where each **vas deferens**
meets its **seminal vesicle**. The **rectum** is drawn behind.
**14 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **The zone proportions are not the real ones.** They are drawn so four zones
  can be told apart. In life the peripheral zone is about seventy per cent of
  the glandular tissue and the transition zone about five. **No volume, ratio or
  zone size may be read off this model.**
- **Zone boundaries are surfaces of revolution and planes.** Real ones are
  neither, and they differ between people and change with age.
- **No disease.** Benign enlargement and cancer are not represented in any form.
  Where they arise is stated as anatomy — which zone — and nothing more.
- **Not present:** the capsule, the neurovascular bundles, the internal and
  external sphincters, the prostatic utricle, the periurethral glands, the
  urethral crest and sinuses, Denonvilliers' fascia, the venous plexus.
- **Not measured**, and not anyone's prostate.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **which zone is where**, relative to the urethra, the
ejaculatory ducts and the rectum, and that is what the tests fix: the peripheral
zone encloses the other two and is the surface nearest the rectum; the
transition zone is in front of and below the central zone; the anterior stroma
is the front; the urethra passes through the gland rather than beside it and
runs from the bladder neck to the apex; both ejaculatory ducts end at the
verumontanum and lie inside the central zone.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **Three zones are inside other zones, and none is moved to be seen.** The
  slider fades the outer zones inwards so the inner ones and then the lumens
  appear; the viewpoints come at the gland from the sides the relations are on;
  isolation from the list takes one zone out of the four.
- **The peripheral zone is drawn double-sided**, because a shell that has been
  faded is being looked at from inside.
- **A "From behind, the rectal surface" viewpoint exists**, because that is the
  side the peripheral zone is on and the side an examining finger is on.
- **The bladder neck and rectum are context**, excluded from the framing
  subject. They are drawn so the gland is between two things rather than
  floating — which is the whole of why it can obstruct.
- **Colour separates the four zones**; "Natural tissue" puts glandular colour
  back and leaves the anterior stroma distinguishable, because it is not gland.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — biopsy, resection, radiotherapy or surgery included. It carries no
score, no threshold and no test result.
