## 1. What question this model answers

**Which part of the lung am I looking at, what is it called in English and
Japanese, and what is it connected to?**

## 2. What it is

An interactive gross-anatomy model of the two lungs, built in code. The
parenchyma is carved into **five lobes** by the oblique and horizontal
fissures; the bronchial tree runs from the trachea through both main bronchi to
five lobar and **eighteen segmental bronchi**; the pulmonary arteries run with
the bronchi and the pulmonary veins run between the segments instead. **65
structures are selectable by name**, drawn from 77 meshes.

Three files are what a reader is being told by, and all three are in the
revision registry: the organ builder decides the shape, the naming adapter
decides what each mesh is called, and the scene decides what a click selects
and what an isolated view hides. The shape they are held to is
[`src/app/anatomyContract.js`](../../src/app/anatomyContract.js).

A **bronchopulmonary segment** here is the lung nearest one segmental bronchus,
inside one lobe. That is the definition applied, not a specimen traced, and it
is why the boundaries are smooth where real ones are not.

## 3. What it is not

- **Not a measured airway.** Calibres, lengths and branching angles are drawn to
  read clearly. Real branching is markedly asymmetric where this is regular, and
  a lung has twenty-three generations where this has four.
- **Not a specimen.** No pleura, no lymphatics, no bronchial (systemic)
  circulation, no alveoli, no cardiac impression beyond the left cardiac notch.
- **Not a ventilation or perfusion model.** Nothing here is solved. The scene
  has no state, no gas exchange and no numbers.
- **Not a segment-by-segment mesh.** The eighteen segments are addressable
  through their bronchi and arteries, which exist as geometry. The parenchyma is
  divided into lobes, not into eighteen separate solids.

## 4. Sources and licence

All geometry is generated by this repository from the division scheme in
`lungAnatomy.js` (Jackson–Huber segment numbering and the standard fissure
planes, stated there as normals and positions in an anatomical frame). No
external 3D asset, no imaging, no patient data. Nothing is redistributed, so
there is no third-party licence attached to this scene.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the tests hold:

- Five lobes whose union is the parenchyma and whose volumes sit in the expected
  proportions.
- Eighteen segments, ten right and eight left, each inside its own lobe.
- The right main bronchus shorter, wider and more steeply set than the left —
  **the ordering only**. The ratio is understated at about 1 : 1.13 against a
  real 1 : 2, because both lungs are placed symmetrically and no heart displaces
  the left hilum. `docs/medical-notes.md` records this.
- RALS at the hilum: the four structures cross the mediastinal surface in the
  arrangement the acronym names.
- The left hilum higher than the right.

What is not held: any individual airway or vessel dimension.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The scene says what frame shape it needs.** Both lungs together fill the
  frame's width at an aspect of 0.72, so a narrow window pulls the camera back
  rather than cropping them.
- **The anatomical-layer slider fades; it does not dissect.** At 0 the
  parenchyma is solid. Past the middle it drops to about 8% opacity and the
  trees inside come up *in place* — nothing is moved, because a bronchus lifted
  out of the lung is no longer where it is.
- **Colour is a viewing aid.** "Lobes and vessels" separates the five lobes and
  the three tree types; "Natural tissue" puts one tissue colour back on each.
  Neither is measured tissue colour and neither changes geometry.
- **Nothing breathes.** The builder's excursion is set to zero: this is anatomy,
  and a lung that inflates while a reader is trying to point at a segmental
  bronchus is moving the target. `breathing-lungs` is where a breath lives.
- **The section view cuts.** It is deliberately different from the slider's
  fade and from a viewpoint that removes one lung, and a reader is meant to be
  able to tell the three apart.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice. In the other order — isolating something already hidden, by
  the reader or by the viewpoint — **isolation wins outright**: it is a
  temporary override that writes nothing down, so clearing it hands back the
  viewpoint and the hidden set exactly as they were. Resolving the three as an
  order rather than as one "or" is what keeps a model on screen at all; OR'd,
  isolating a structure the viewpoint was already hiding hid every structure in
  the scene.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — bronchoscopy, biopsy, resection or localisation included. The
airways are not measured and the segment boundaries are a model of a
definition.
