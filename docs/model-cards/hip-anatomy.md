## 1. What question this model answers

**Why does this joint keep both its range and its stability, when the shoulder
and the knee each give one of them up?**

## 2. What it is

A **right hip**, standing. The **hip bone** — ilium, pubis and ischium fused
into one — carries the **acetabulum**, and the acetabulum is a *cup*: its rim
reaches past the widest part of the head, so the head cannot come straight out.
The **acetabular labrum** rings its mouth. Inside sits the **head of the
femur**, held out to the side on a **neck**, with the **greater** and **lesser
trochanters** where the neck meets the **shaft**.

**Articular cartilage** covers the head almost all over and lines the inside of
the cup. The **ligament of the head** crosses the floor of the socket and holds
nothing. Round the joint are the three capsular ligaments — the
**iliofemoral**, the strongest in the body, the **pubofemoral** and the
**ischiofemoral** — and across it run the two tendons that explain the two
trochanters: **gluteus medius** to the greater, **iliopsoas** to the lesser.
**15 structures are selectable.**

## 3. What it is not

- **Nothing moves.** One position, standing. No ligament tightens or slackens
  and the head does not translate.
- **No dimension is a measurement — the neck–shaft angle included.** It is
  drawn to read. It is not this model's place to say what that angle is in
  anyone.
- **The capsule itself is not drawn.** Its three named thickenings are drawn as
  separate bands, because a bag round the joint hides everything the scene
  exists to show; and the iliofemoral ligament is drawn as one band where in
  life it is an inverted Y with two limbs.
- **No disease.** No fracture, arthritis, impingement, dysplasia or dislocation
  in any form. That the neck is the hip's weak point is stated as anatomy and
  nothing more.
- **Not present:** the acetabular notch and its transverse ligament, the
  horseshoe shape of the acetabular cartilage, the bursae, the remaining hip
  muscles, the retinacular vessels of the head and neck, the sciatic nerve.
- **One hip, the right one**, and not anyone's.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the shape of the grip and what crosses it**, and that
is what the tests fix: ball and cup are drawn about one point; the labral rim
reaches past the centre of the head but not so far as to swallow it; the labrum
is a ring in the plane of the socket's mouth; head, neck and greater trochanter
lie in that order going outwards and the head is above the shaft; gluteus medius
ends on the greater trochanter and iliopsoas on the lesser; the iliofemoral and
pubofemoral ligaments cross the front and the ischiofemoral the back, each
reaching from the hip bone to the femur; the ligament of the head stays inside
the socket; and the two cartilage layers lie between the two bones and nowhere
else.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The section is where the claim lives.** "A rim that reaches past the widest
  part of the head" is a sentence until the joint is cut in half, so the scene
  has a coronal section — and it *cuts*, rather than pulling the femur out of
  the socket.
- **"The socket, with the femur put away"** hides the femur by tag. The
  cartilage goes with it, because one of its two meshes has the shape of the
  head.
- **The slider fades the bones**, so the ligament of the head — which is inside
  the joint and can otherwise only be seen in section — comes into view without
  anything being moved.
- **The acetabulum is drawn as its own shell**, not as a face of the hip bone,
  so that a rim and an inside exist to point at.
- **Screen-left is the patient's right** (`docs/architecture-rules.md` rule 5),
  so in this right hip medial is `+x`; every side comes from one `MEDIAL`
  constant.
- **Taking a structure out of the way is the reader's own way in.** The layer
  slider, and where this scene has them the cuts and the viewpoints, are its
  *authored* ways of showing what is inside; hiding is the one the reader
  chooses — one structure, or a whole branch of the part tree in a single
  press. It asserts nothing anatomical and moves nothing: a hidden structure is
  still there, still listed, still findable by name, and **Unhide all** brings
  every one of them back. While it is off screen it also leaves the picker, so
  a ray cannot select what the reader cannot see. Hiding the structure that is
  currently isolated ends the isolation, because "only this one" and "not this
  one" cannot both be true, and that ending is announced rather than left for a
  panel to notice.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — injection, arthroscopy, fracture fixation or arthroplasty included.
It carries no grade, no angle to measure and no threshold.
