## 1. What question this model answers

**Which tissue is it in, how deep is it, and which way does it drain?**

## 2. What it is

A **right breast**. **Skin**, **areola** and **nipple** outside; **adipose
tissue** making up most of the bulk; eight **lactiferous duct** systems all
converging on the nipple, each ending in **lobules**; **Cooper's ligaments**
running from the chest-wall side through the gland to the skin; **pectoralis
major** behind everything; and the **axillary tail** running out to the
**axillary nodes**. **10 structures are selectable.**

`ductal`, `lobular`, `axillary` and `chest-wall` are tags as well as
structures, because those four words are how the subject is divided everywhere
else.

## 3. What it is not

- **The outline is deliberately plain.** A breast modelled for its shape is an
  illustration; what earns its place here is the arrangement inside, and the
  outline exists so the arrangement has somewhere to be.
- **The counts are display counts** (`DISPLAY_COUNTS`). A breast has fifteen to
  twenty duct systems and far more lobules. **No count may be read off this
  model.**
- **Nothing moves or changes.** Not with age, not with the cycle, not with
  pregnancy; **breast density is not represented**.
- **The node markers are markers for a group**, not models of nodes. One node
  at its own scale is `lymph-node-anatomy`.
- **Not present:** the internal mammary drainage, the quadrants a report is
  written in, the retromammary space, the ribs, the muscles behind the
  pectoralis, the blood supply, the nerves.
- **No disease**, and not anyone's breast.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is **the shape of the tree, the order of the depths and
the direction of drainage**, and that is what the tests fix: every duct reaches
the nipple and no lobule does; the lobules are deeper than the ducts' near
ends; skin is outside fat, the gland is inside fat, the muscle is behind all of
it and nothing glandular is inside the muscle; Cooper's ligaments span from the
chest-wall side out to the skin; and the tail runs from the upper gland towards
the armpit with the nodes beyond it.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The slider fades skin and fat**, so the gland is reached without the breast
  being opened.
- **A lateral view exists**, because "does it move with the chest wall" is a
  question about depth and depth cannot be seen from the front.
- **The ducts view hides the surface and the ligaments by tag**, so the one
  thing worth seeing — every duct arriving at one place — is not behind
  anything.
- **Ducts and lobules are separate structures** with separate tags, because that
  division is what the whole subject is named by.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, staging, screening
decisions, or planning any biopsy or surgery.
