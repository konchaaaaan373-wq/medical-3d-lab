## 1. What question this model answers

**Where is the trigone, and what are its three corners?**

## 2. What it is

The same bladder shape `kidney-anatomy` draws at the end of the tract, cut into
**apex**, **body**, **fundus** and **neck**. Inside the base is the **trigone**,
with a **ureteric orifice** at each upper corner and the **internal urethral
orifice** at the lower one; the two **ureters** arrive at the first two and the
**urethra** leaves from the third. **11 structures are selectable.**

The slider makes the wall translucent, because the trigone cannot be seen from
outside at all.

## 3. What it is not

- **The trigone has no thickness.** It is drawn as a flat patch on the inner
  surface — which is what a region of lining is — and the mucosal folds it is
  smooth *by contrast with* are not drawn either.
- **The bladder is at one fixed degree of filling.** Nothing fills and nothing
  empties. `kidney-anatomy`'s bladder is the one that changes shape.
- **Not measured.** Every dimension is schematic.
- **Not present:** the oblique intramural course of the ureters (described in
  the copy, not modelled), the internal and external sphincters, the detrusor's
  layers, the urachal remnant, the peritoneum over the dome, and the prostate or
  vagina beneath the neck.
- **The urethra is drawn the same for everyone.** Male and female urethral
  anatomy differ in length and in what surrounds them, and neither is
  represented.

## 4. Sources and licence

Generated by this repository. No external asset, no imaging, no patient data.

## 5. Accuracy and uncertainty

What the model claims is arrangement: the four wall parts partition the organ;
the trigone is on the inner surface of the base; its two upper corners are the
ureteric orifices and its lower corner the internal urethral orifice; each
ureter ends at its own orifice and the urethra begins at the third. Those are
held in `tests/organ-parts-anatomy.test.js`, and the naming through the anatomy
contract in `tests/organ-anatomy-scenes.test.js`.

No dimension here is a measurement.

## 6. Presentation choices

- **The opening view deliberately does not look at the trigone.** It is on the
  back wall and it is found by fading the front; a "Posterior (the base)"
  viewpoint is what it is read from, and every structure on the inside names it.
- **The trigone is drawn double-sided**, because half the readers who look for
  it will be looking at its back.
- **The ureters and urethra are context**, excluded from the framing subject.
- **Colour separates the four wall parts**; "Natural tissue" puts one bladder
  colour back.
- **The frame follows the viewpoint.** The camera fits the organ into the part
  of the window nothing is covering — the parts panel is an overlay over the
  canvas, not a narrower canvas — and a viewpoint that hides a side or cuts the
  organ open is framed on what it leaves rather than on the whole model.
- **A cut draws the face it leaves, where there is one to draw.** For a solid
  part the cross-section is computed from the triangles the plane crosses and
  drawn in that part's own colour, so a cut segment reads as tissue with a
  surface rather than as a shell seen from the inside. For a hollow one — a
  stomach, a duct, a loop of bowel — the cut opens it instead: the wall here is
  a surface with no thickness, and facing it would draw a lumen as a lump of
  tissue. Which a part is, is declared by the scene rather than guessed.
- **Isolating a structure shows it solid.** "Show me only this" used to leave
  the part at the opacity it has in place, which is right for the opaque ones
  and useless for the see-through ones — and a structure you cannot see in
  place is exactly the one a reader isolates. Nothing else is drawn, so there
  is nothing left for it to be transparent against.

## 7. What it must never be used for

Diagnosis, treatment selection, dose selection, prognosis, or planning any
procedure — cystoscopy, catheterisation, resection or stenting included.
